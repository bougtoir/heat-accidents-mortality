# IJBM Hostile Pre-Submission Review

Each criticism, whether it required revision, and the resolution.

1. **Novelty vs Wu et al. 2018 and newer studies.** Valid — the old framing ("rarely studied", "under-recognised") was untenable given Wu 2018, Pan 2024, Hsu 2025/2026. **Revised**: Introduction opens by acknowledging the established literature; contribution reframed as anomaly exposure + lag decomposition + attributable burden + road-user heterogeneity in nationwide fatal crashes + transportability assessment.
2. **Does the temperature anomaly add information?** Defensible — it separates "unusual for season/place" from the seasonal exposure envelope; Discussion now argues this explicitly and notes complementarity to heat-wave threshold designs.
3. **+9 °C contrast choice.** Partially valid — addressed by new Table S1: same-day/cumulative RR at +5 °C (1.079/1.031), empirical p95 anomaly 6.25 °C (1.104/1.043), +9 °C (1.165/1.076). Interpretation is contrast-robust; the continuous curve remains primary.
4. **Ecological exposure assignment.** Real limitation — retained and stated: state-level nearest-station exposure misclassifies individuals and misses urban heat islands. Not fixable with current data.
5. **Travel-volume denominator.** Real — national monthly VMT/gasoline and annual state VMT are coarse; stated as a limitation; sensitivity models show the association survives them.
6. **Mode-specific denominators.** Real — no public daily pedestrian/cyclist/motorcycle counts; the open-air gradient may partly reflect weather-related activity. Explicitly stated in Results and Discussion; mechanism language downgraded to "compatible with".
7. **Residual confounding.** Acknowledged (heat-stress metrics tested individually with VIF screening; precipitation controlled). Not fully resolvable ecologically — stated.
8. **Lag interpretation.** The 1–3 d deficit is labelled displacement/harvesting; cumulative RR still >1. Text now says "partial short-term displacement", not "deaths avoided".
9. **Multiple subgroup comparisons.** All subgroup outputs labelled exploratory/hypothesis-generating, not adjusted for multiple comparisons — in captions, tables and text.
10. **Japan non-replication.** Not suppressed: reported as exploratory external transportability, kept concise in main text with detail in supplement; plausible explanations flagged as hypotheses only. Prior Japanese evidence (Pan 2024) is cited so the null is contextualised, not anomalous.
11. **Causal language.** Swept: "associated with"/"compatible with" throughout; Conclusion states ecological design cannot establish individual causation.
12. **Attributable-burden assumptions.** Rests on the fitted association applying to the anomaly distribution and on harvesting being correctly netted; flagged as model-based burden, not a coded count.
13. **Comparison with coded heat deaths.** Both retained and reframed: juxtaposed "for magnitude context", with explicit statement that the constructs differ; moved out of headline claims (Fig. 5 main, Fig. S3 supplement).
14. **Warming-scenario assumptions.** Reframed as "illustrative scenario translation" holding population/activity/fleet/adaptation constant; details moved to Fig. S4/Table S3 with a single sentence in Results; never called a forecast.
15. **Generalisability.** US-only inference; Japan uncertain; CARE/Europe data gap explained as a scope limitation.
16. **Fit for IJBM.** Reframed to biometeorology: seasonal-anomaly exposure, heat-health burden, human–environment interaction, adaptation relevance; road-safety engineering content de-emphasised.

## Residual risks accepted (documented, not fixable within scope)

- Ecological design is the study's inherent limit; reviewers may still ask for individual-level linkage — acknowledged as future work.
